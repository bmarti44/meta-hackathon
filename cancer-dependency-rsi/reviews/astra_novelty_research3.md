**What most strongly challenges the framing**

1. **The original Broad study already performs genome-wide format analysis.** Extended Data Fig. 4g–h compares 55 dome-grown with 14 coat-grown organoids: correlation 0.984, with all differential-test q-values above 0.71. These are unmatched model groups. Fig. 5h/Extended Data Fig. 10a individually validate MSMO1 and SQLE effects. The paper also discusses lost ESR1/ERBB2 dependencies. Consequently, neither genome-wide format comparison nor attention to dependency losses is new here. [Neiswender et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10843-7)

2. **The minipool denominator matters.** The released materials distinguish 250 selected test genes from 650 negative and 100 positive controls. Counting significant entries in the released test tables gives eight medium-associated genes—PTK2, SPTLC1, CTNNA1, SPTLC2, PPP1R15B, RICTOR, SQLE and PPP2R2D—and seven format-associated genes. That is **not** a fraction of all organoid-specific dependencies. MSMO1 is supported by the separate validation assays, rather than significant minipool regression. The medium contrast changes whole formulations, OPAC versus RPMI–FBS; it does not isolate serum alone. [Released data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.29472362)

3. **Genome-wide medium-associated correction is established resource practice.** Sanger’s documentation explicitly describes removal of a medium-associated principal component from integrated **cell-line** data. This must be credited, while recognizing that PC removal neither identifies a purely causal medium component nor supplies the proposed organoid target flags. [Sanger documentation](https://depmap.sanger.ac.uk/documentation/datasets/wg-crispr-knockout/), [Pacini et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-21898-7)

4. **Serum-free genome-scale comparisons exist beyond Rossiter.** Liang is a direct precedent, but its abstract does not establish catalogue scale or publish attribution fractions. Metz supplies another genome-wide medium comparison, with serum present in both conditions. I found no verified hundreds-model factorial serum/serum-free catalogue matching the proposed analysis. [Liang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/neuped/wuaf001.127), [Metz et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-026-12658-2)

**Saturation point:** after identifying the Sanger/Pacini correction precedent, repeated attribution-, fraction-, correction-, author-, repository- and citation-focused searches stopped producing new A/B sources. I therefore reached **practical saturation for publicly discoverable evidence bearing on the narrow novelty claim**, not universal saturation of all organoid literature.

**Coverage limits and confidence**

- Both Nature papers were inspected using accessible web material and local copies of their primary full-text XML.
- Supplementary data inspection included cached primary Broad files and the Sanger ten-table archive. **Not every supplementary spreadsheet cell was audited.**
- Some supplementary narrative and peer-review PDFs could not be retrieved. Complete text was also unavailable for selected preprints and the large gastric-organoid PDF; these limitations constrain negative conclusions about their contents.
- Citation chasing used indexed DOI/title references. A complete forward-citation export was not obtained from citation APIs.
- Very recent October publications, unindexed repositories, private analyses and unpublished work remain potential gaps.
- **High confidence:** several broad novelty interpretations are overstated.
- **Moderate confidence:** the specific catalogue-wide fractions and per-gene medium-consistency annotation remain unreported in discoverable literature through **2026-10-30**.
