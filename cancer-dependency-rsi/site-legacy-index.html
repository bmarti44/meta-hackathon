An independent audit of the 2026 organoid dependency catalogues — DepMap 26Q3

Is it the cancer,
or is it the menu?

In 2026, two teams published genome-wide maps of genes that mini-tumours grown in dishes ("organoids") cannot live without — a goldmine of candidate drug targets. But organoids are fed a defined, serum-free diet that blood never resembles. We re-ran the numbers every way the public data allow and found that about 85% of the "organoid-specific" weak spots follow the food, not the 3D shape — including most of one catalogue's headline drug targets. This site lets anyone check any of 17,525 genes in seconds.

02

Findings

1 · The food explains most of it — gains and losses alike

Of 456 genes whose essentiality differs between organoids and conventional cultures, 86% of gains and 84% of losses are reproduced in flat-grown cells fed organoid medium (75–82% under conservative robust statistics). The strongest class is cholesterol/sterol manufacture (SQLE, MSMO1), followed by fatty-acid and heme synthesis — exactly the pathways a serum-free, lipid-poor diet would stress. Gene-expression data show the mirror image (cholesterol genes switched on in organoid medium, the classic starvation response), and in independent screens using blood-like medium, the flagship gene SQLE moves the opposite way.

2 · Auditing the published target lists

Against the Broad catalogue's own published hits: 47% are medium-consistent overall, and 87% of those that replicate under lineage adjustment. Against Sanger's "organoid-specific core fitness" headline-18: 8 of 16 replicate here, 5 are medium-consistent — four of them the SREBP/sterol axis (MVD, IDI1, MBTPS1, MBTPS2) — and 3 remain unresolved (SCAP, DDX3X, ITGB1), making them the more credible follow-up candidates. Sanger's own calls are prevalent in their assay (median depletion in 70–80% of organoids); the issue is attribution, not call quality.

3 · The weakening that isn't (mostly) biology

Ribosome and spliceosome genes look less essential in organoids. With curated gene sets, only the ribosomal set shows a clear whole-complex shift (+0.17 vs +0.03 baseline), present in both labs' assays after global corrections; Sanger's organoid screens are globally shallower (essential depth 0.63×). Slower growth → fewer cell divisions → long-lived protein machines don't have time to wear out is the leading explanation — but it's a hypothesis: cumulative doublings are recorded for only 1 of 1,241 Broad screens, so nobody can settle it from this data release.

What would settle it

Add LDL cholesterol back to organoid medium. If the sterol dependencies are starvation, SQLE-inhibitor and statin sensitivity should collapse (≥5× potency shift) while genuine candidates like ITGB1 don't budge. Pre-agreed falsification: if sensitivity survives LDL repletion in ≥3 of 4 models, our interpretation fails. The full protocol is in the wet-lab proposal (Downloads) — a ~2-week experiment, no new screen needed.

03

How this was made

An honest changelog — including the mistakes. This audit was produced by an AI-assisted research programme with human direction, then attacked by an independent AI reviewer five times before release.

  1. 2026 · The catalogues

    Two Nature papers map organoid weak spots

    Broad (Neiswender et al.) and Sanger (Herranz-Ors et al.) publish genome-wide CRISPR dependency maps for patient-derived organoids, nominating organoid-specific drug targets. Broad's own 1,000-gene side experiment already hints some hits are medium-driven — but nobody counts, genome-wide, across both catalogues.

  2. The design

    Four comparisons that separate food from shape

    Public data already contain the controls: organoid medium grown flat (food without shape), domes vs flat within the same food family (shape without food change), and an unrelated serum-free brain-cancer diet. Add corrections for the two different CRISPR toolkits used (measured from 22 models screened with both), cancer-type mix, and screen strength — and hold out entire patients to check the answer on unseen data.

  3. Round 1

    First pass: 85% food, 0% shape

    The headline landed immediately, including a dramatic "spliceosome collapse" (+0.9). Confidence was high. Too high.

  4. Self-correction

    The spliceosome that wasn't

    Swapping a small hand-picked gene panel for the full curated sets shrank the spliceosome effect from +0.90 to +0.02. The panel had been cherry-picked by history. The ribosome effect survived; the spliceosome story was demoted in print, in the abstract, everywhere.

  5. Triangulation

    Three independent witnesses

    Gene-expression data show the cholesterol-starvation mirror image (+0.36 vs −0.38). Sanger's independent assay shows the same cholesterol gain (percentile 0.099 vs 0.394). Blood-like medium screens from 2021 move SQLE the opposite way. Different labs, different methods, same conclusion.

  6. Adversarial review

    Five rounds against a skeptical reviewer

    An independent AI reviewer (Codex, highest reasoning) audited code and claims, recomputing from source. It found real faults every round — contaminated calibration, an impossible statistical test for shape effects, a bootstrap that estimated a different quantity than claimed, a NaN bug corrupting one gene's stability score — each verified, fixed, and re-reviewed.

  7. Novelty audit

    "Hasn't somebody done this?" — 230+ web searches later

    A separate uncapped deep-research run hunted for prior art. Verdict: partially true — the general ideas have real precedents (2019–2026, now cited), one web report claiming similar scope turned out to carry a fabricated arXiv ID, and a commentary nine days earlier asked the same question without numbers.   8. Now

    Over to the wet lab

    Everything computational that can be squeezed from public data has been — twice-checked. What remains is a two-week experiment: add the cholesterol back. The proposal, with falsification criteria agreed in advance, is in the downloads.

04

Methods

In plain language

For every gene we ask four questions of the public data: (1) Does its importance change in organoids vs conventional cultures? (2) Does the same change happen in flat-grown cells fed organoid medium? (3) Is there anything left over when comparing blobs to flat on the same food? (4) Does an unrelated serum-free diet do something similar? If (2) reproduces most of (1) and (3) is small, we call the gene medium-consistent. Mirror-image logic gives format candidate; everything else is honestly unresolved. These are consistency labels from observational data — not causal proof. Proof needs the add-back experiment.

Technical details

DepMap 26Q3 Chronos gene effects (PassesQC screens, Avana + Humagne-CD), development patients only for all calibration, filtering, and imputation. Per-gene library offset from 22 development FBS models screened with both libraries (all Avana screens are FBS). Per-gene OLS on model-mean Chronos with lineage dummies; Benjamini–Hochberg q per contrast; HC3 robust SEs shipped and used for sensitivity. Hits: D q<0.01 and |Δ|≥0.25 (dynamic-range-normalized scale primary; raw concordant). Medium-consistent: same-sign adherent ratio ≥0.5, nominal p<0.05, 90% delta-method CI excluding 0, format not dominant under mirror criteria; strict-FDR variant released as flag_fdr. Joint bootstrap (B=150) shares one model resample between calibration and analysis; identity replicate reproduces point flags exactly. Held-out evaluation is fully disjoint (25 reserved-patient domes vs 78 reserved-patient FBS controls). Common-support sensitivity restricts to the six lineages with adherent comparators (38 domes): slope 0.89. Humagne-only sensitivity filters at screen level (7 FBS controls, r=0.78). Sanger: supplementary tables 4/5/6 and cell-line EssMatrix; within-model percentiles are primary across differently processed matrices. Expression: Omics TPM logp1 mapped by ModelConditionID. HPLM: Rossiter et al. Table S1 scaled gene scores. Full code in Downloads.

Limitations, stated plainly

Adherent comparators: 9 models, 6 of 9 lineages, mixed (not identical) formulations — per-gene verdicts carry real uncertainty (use the bootstrap frequencies). Model derivation/selection differences are not excluded by lineage adjustment. Library handling rests on 22 dual-screened models. Cumulative doublings are recorded for 1 of 1,241 Broad screens, so the weakening mechanism is unresolved. The 2026 catalogues themselves were not re-screened; all inference is from their public data. What this site claims is an audit with quantified consistency — nothing more.
